PA3 — Ventilator Pressure Prediction

DSC 102 — Systems for Scalable Analytics  |  Fall 2026  |  Due: November 16, 2026
Team member: Matthew Lee

I spent approximately 6–8 hours on this assignment, working through data exploration, feature engineering, and several rounds of training and submission. I used LightGBM with 3-fold GroupKFold cross-validation grouped by breath_id, training only on inspiratory rows since those are what the leaderboard scores. I went through a few iterations — starting with a basic baseline, then adding physics-informed features derived from the lung mechanics equation P = R·(dV/dt) + V/C, and finally making the model auto-regressive by feeding the previous time step's pressure back in as a feature. That last change was by far the most impactful, dropping cross-validation MAE from around 0.45 to around 0.13. Claude Code helped design the physics features, debug a subtle early stopping issue, and build the step-by-step auto-regressive test inference loop, though it couldn't predict in advance how much each change would actually help — I had to run the full training to find out. The biggest thing I learned is that for sequential prediction, giving the model access to recent values of the target is almost always more valuable than any amount of input feature engineering. I also learned why MSE converges faster than MAE as a training objective in gradient-boosted trees: MAE's gradient is always ±1 regardless of error size, while MSE's gradient scales with the residual, making large errors train faster. If I had more time, I would experiment with adding a lightweight recurrent model on top of the LightGBM predictions to better capture the temporal structure within each breath sequence.
