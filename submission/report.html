1. Problem Overview

The goal of PA3 was to predict airway pressure in a mechanical ventilator simulation given control signals (u_in, u_out) and lung parameters (R — resistance, C — compliance). The evaluation metric was mean absolute error (MAE) computed only on inspiratory rows where u_out == 0. The dataset contained 5.4 million training rows across roughly 67,500 breath sequences.

2. Approach

I used LightGBM with GroupKFold cross-validation (3 folds, grouped by breath_id) to prevent data leakage across breath sequences. The model was trained exclusively on inspiratory rows — the same subset that the leaderboard scores — which both focused learning on the target metric and reduced training time by roughly 50%.

3. Feature Engineering

The most important design decision was grounding the feature set in the physics of mechanical ventilation. The governing equation for airway pressure is:

P ≈ R · (dV/dt) + V / C

where V is the lung volume (the time-integral of flow) and dV/dt ≈ u_in (the valve control signal). This equation produces two interpretable feature groups:

  • P_resist = R · u_in — the resistive pressure component due to airflow
  • P_compliance = cumsum(u_in · Δt) / C — the elastic pressure component from accumulated lung volume
  • P_model = P_resist + P_compliance — a direct physics-based pressure prediction

By giving LightGBM a nearly correct physics prediction as a feature, the model only needs to learn small residual corrections (nonlinear valve behavior, end-of-breath transients, etc.) rather than inferring the entire pressure signal from scratch. Additional features included:

  • Lag and lead values of u_in and P_model (up to 20 steps)
  • Rolling means over windows of 3, 5, 10, and 20 time steps
  • First and second differences of u_in
  • Breath-level aggregates: mean, max, std, and total of u_in
  • Lung parameter interactions: R·C, R/C
  • Time-within-breath features: position, duration, cumulative volume

5. Results

An initial baseline submission (without physics features, trained on all rows) scored 0.608 on the leaderboard. After incorporating the physics-informed features and restricting training to inspiratory rows, the model improved substantially. The final leaderboard score is shown in the accompanying screenshot.

The key lesson from this assignment was that domain knowledge — specifically the respiratory mechanics equation — dramatically reduced the learning burden on LightGBM. Instead of learning to map raw valve signals to pressure, the model learned corrections to an already physically meaningful prediction, which is a much easier task.
